# M1 — The World Beneath

**Goal:** a simulated tectonic world, and a region-sized window cut out of it.

M0 proved the instrument. M1 builds the thing every later stage stands on: a
coarse world where plates move, collide, subduct and rift, producing crust that
has a *type*, an *age* and a *thickness* — and then picks the piece of it you're
actually going to live in.

No elevation at 1 km yet. That's M2.

This document is the argument and the map. **The work is specified in seven
sub-milestones, one document each**, because M1 as a single milestone covered a
plate simulation, a config system, logging, a renderer overhaul, region scoring
and a layer architecture — which breaks the rule from [m0.md](m0.md) that each
milestone ends in one PNG you can look at and judge.

> **This supersedes [plan.md](plan.md)'s M1.** That section said "at regional
> scale... there is nothing to simulate — author it instead," and it was right
> about the *scale* and wrong about the *conclusion*. Elevation and sea level
> move to M2 and everything downstream shifts by one.

## Why not simulate at region scale

Worth being clear about, rather than taking on faith.

A tectonic plate is thousands of kilometres across. A region-sized window — call
it 500 km — sits inside one plate, or spans one boundary. Tectonics is the
behaviour of plates *interacting*; if only one plate is in frame there are no
interactions, and anything you "simulate" is authoring with extra steps. You'd
write the mountain range and then pretend you'd discovered it.

So: **simulate at world scale, then crop.**

### But *world* need not mean *planet*

Use a **flat plane that wraps in one axis** — a cylinder — rather than a sphere.

A sphere costs you spherical Voronoi for the plate partition, Euler poles and
rotation matrices instead of velocity vectors, and a geodesic grid instead of
`Grid<T>`. That is a large amount of work, and *none of it is visible inside a
500 km crop*. A cylinder keeps plate motion as 2-D translation and reuses
`hex.rs` unchanged but for a `rem_euclid`.

The cylinder has poles where a sphere doesn't. That's fine: keep the region away
from the top and bottom rows, which region scoring prefers anyway.

## The scale rule

The pipeline now has a **tectonic** grid and a **region** grid. Region size is a
tunable, so don't hard-code a ratio — hard-code the *rule*:

> **The region should span 20–60 tectonic cells.**

Below about 8, the tectonic setting is uniform across the region and you have
gained nothing over authoring it. Above about 100, you're asking the plate sim
for detail it cannot legitimately produce — that detail is fBm's job at M2 and
erosion's at M4. Everything else follows.

| | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Tectonic cell | 16 km | |
| Tectonic grid | 1024 × 512 | 16,384 × 8,192 km — about 26% of Earth's surface area |
| Plates | 6–12 | Earth-proportionate for that area; majors 200–400 cells across |
| Region | ~500 km at 1 km/cell | spans ~32 tectonic cells, comfortably inside the rule |

The region's 30% simulated margin from [plan.md](plan.md) is unchanged and is
cut from the tectonic world along with the rest.

### Why this order

**Logging and the renderer come first because they are how you will look at
everything else.** M1d in particular is close to undebuggable without a
per-frame render and a per-step log.

**Config comes last, after tectonics rather than before it.** An earlier draft of
this document put it in the housekeeping block up front; that was wrong. You
cannot design a config schema for parameters that don't exist yet, and the plate
sim is where the parameters come from. Develop M1d–M1f on CLI flags and
constants, then write the schema against knobs you have actually turned.

**Rejecting a world moves to M1g with it**, for the same reason: re-rolling is
only safe once `params.toml` records which attempt was accepted. Until then a
bare seed is not a durable address for a world. That also suits the discipline
point — write no rejection criteria until you have generated twenty worlds and
looked at them — which by M1g you will have.

## The thing to keep in mind throughout

**Tectonics sits upstream of everything.** Retuning it invalidates every
downstream reference world you have grown attached to. This is the cost of the
decision at the top of this document, taken deliberately in exchange for
mountains that have a reason to be where they are.

Two consequences. Get M1d and M1e into a state you like and then leave them
alone. And when saving stages eventually gets built — deferred past M1, see
[M1g](m1g-config.md) — the tectonic world is the first thing worth persisting,
not because it is slow but because "world Urist, region (410, 180)" ought to be
an address that still means something in a year.

Then start M2, and turn all of this into a coastline.
